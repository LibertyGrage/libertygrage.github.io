What the pack fixes

GTA IV: The Complete Edition is a great game with a few long-standing problems, and FusionFix already fixes many of them. The pack adds fixes for what we kept running into with a modded game: the city failing to load after long trips, and crashes caused by the game and the trainer meeting in unusual ways. Each fix checks the exact game build before it changes anything, and logs what it did.

The city stops loading

What you see: after a long drive, a teleport or a mission cutscene, the ground, buildings or the radar don't appear, or appear blurry, and the game seems to wait for them forever.

Why it happens: GTA IV loads the city into a memory area of a fixed size. Over a long session that area breaks into many small pieces, and a big piece of the map no longer fits anywhere, even though your PC has plenty of memory left.

What the pack does: it gives that area much more room before the game creates it, and lets the game stream more of the city at once. If your game is short of memory space, it holds back automatically.

Crash: a car hit on a part it doesn't have

What you see: the game closes during a fight or a crash involving an add-on or replaced car.

Why it happens: some car models leave out parts the game expects, and when that missing part is hit, the game reads data that isn't there.

What the pack does: it makes the game skip a hit on a part the model doesn't have, after checking that it is patching exactly the Complete Edition 1.2.0.59 code it expects.

Crash: the bodyguard menu

What you see: the game closes while you use the trainer's bodyguard menu, often after a fight.

Why it happens: a bodyguard who died and despawned can stay on the trainer's list, and the menu then talks to someone who no longer exists.

What the pack does: it checks that each bodyguard still exists before the trainer reaches for them.

Crash: a bodyguard with the player's model

What you see: the game closes the moment a new bodyguard spawns.

Why it happens: the first entry in the trainer's model list is the player's own model, and the game crashes when anyone else is given it.

What the pack does: it blocks that one model for bodyguards.

The seat belt switches itself off

The trainer applies its seat belt only when its settings load or when you toggle it, so the game can quietly switch it off. The pack keeps applying it, so it stays on.

Menus that are hard to read

The pack marks the selected line in the trainer's menus in the theme's own colour, even on lines with their own colours, so you always know where you are.

How the fixes stay safe

  • Every patch first compares the game's code with what it expects. On any other build it changes nothing and writes why in its log.
  • The pack never edits the game's files on disk: everything happens in memory while the game runs, and removing the pack's files undoes all of it.
